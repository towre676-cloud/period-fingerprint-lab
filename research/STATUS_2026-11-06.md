**Scope:** regularized quantum periods of well-formed terminal weighted
projective spaces; distinguish supported nonzero coefficients from the full
zero-inclusive period series. Not a theorem about all Fano varieties.

## Two general theorems

1. For every fixed finite K, there are distinct terminal well-formed
   weighted projective spaces with identical first K supported
   coefficients and equal total weight. The construction uses the prime
   number theorem and an integer nullspace followed by terminal padding.
   It does not guarantee different dimensions for all K.
2. If total weight L is known, L consecutive supported coefficients
   beginning with the constant term recover the exact weight multiset
   by rational polynomial interpolation and divisor inversion. There
   is also a dense-series corollary recovering L when enough zero-inclusive
   coefficients are supplied. Mathematical priority of this exact
   sufficient bound remains to be assessed against Veneziale's earlier
   factorial-factorization algorithm.

## Verification caveats

- Exact finite-field full rank is a rigorous *rational independence*
  certificate; a modular dependency is not itself an integer relation.
  The K4 example has an actual independent integer reconstruction.
- The K4 witness is astronomical in dimension, so it is not a
  practically small geometric counterexample.
- The reported exhaustive searches are bounded and computer-assisted.
  They are not Lean-verified classification proofs.
- No originality claim is made. The quantum-period framework,
  terminality test and prior truncation reconstruction literature
  have established precursors.
- The complete 150,000-record Zenodo dataset has not yet been
  independently downloaded and audited in this repository.

## Continuation: compact four-coefficient collision and five-coefficient bound

A further independent exact-lattice search through maximum individual
weight 2000 reconstructed 21 rational kernel directions, saturated the
integer row lattice via Hermite normal form and used exact Gram-matrix
LLL to obtain a **much smaller K=4 dimension-changing terminal pair**.
The exact signed multiplicities are at
[k4_compact_vector.b85](k4_compact_vector.b85); the
standalone [verifier](../scripts/verify_k4_compact.py) replays the
integer factorial identities and checks *all* relevant fractional ages.
No padding is necessary.

- Common total weight: **6,448,232**.
- Geometric dimensions: **12,621** and **12,622**.
- Nonzero signed weight types: **706** (maximum weight **1990**).
- First four supported coefficients match; fifth differs at
  **367** prime valuations.
- Exact terminality age ranges: [2,12620] and [2,12621],
  with **zero violations** across **6,448,229** checked
  indices per side.
- Minimal total weight and minimal dimension **not established**.
- Compared with the earlier 1692-max-weight witness, this construction
  trades slightly larger individual weights for vastly smaller
  multiplicities and total weight.

For **five** supported coefficients, a standalone F_65521 full-rank
certificate excludes all distinct, equal-total-weight factorial
multisets with each individual weight at most **25,808**. An
independent packed F_2 checker gives full rank through **25,626**.
Modular dependencies first appear at **25,809** and **25,627**
in those respective fields, but neither by itself proves a
rational or integer collision. An explicit K=5 collision has
not yet been certified.

This continuation is mathematically nontrivial but still
**unrefereed**; no novelty claim is made. See
[K4 compact construction](K4_COMPACT_TERMINAL_COLLISION.md) and
[K5 exact rank bound](K5_FINITE_RANK_BOUND.md).


## Further K5 advance: exact finite prime-count existence (2026-11-06)

The maximum-individual-weight bound for existence of a
**distinct terminal same-dimension five-prefix collision**
has been made completely effective:

```
       25,809 <= M5 <= 2,867,000.
```

- Lower bound: `rank V_5(25808)=25808` over F_65521 and
  independently F_65519. Earlier F_2 rank gives the
  weaker lower bound 25627.
- Upper bound: the exact prime counts `pi(m*2867000)`,
  for m=1,2,3,4,5, are respectively
  `207927,395799,577502,755330,930416`.
  These total **2,866,974**, leaving at least **25**
  rational kernel dimensions among the
  **2,867,000** candidate-weight columns after
  adding the total-weight row.
- Adding an all-ones weight-count row still leaves at
  least **24** independent rational kernel directions.
  An integer count-zero relation therefore exists,
  yielding distinct spaces **of the same dimension**.
- Padding with 5,734,000 common unit weights
  guarantees both spaces are well-formed and terminal,
  and does not increase the largest individual weight.

Both full and odd-only Python sieves, plus an independent
segmented C++ sieve, replay the exact prime counts.
This significantly improves the previous 41.1-billion
upper bound, which used a published analytical
prime-counting inequality.

**The explicit integer relation remains unconstructed.**
The dimension-changing K5 question is still open.
