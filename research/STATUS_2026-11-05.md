# Status of the finite-fingerprint research campaign — 2026-11-05

The current program has three mathematically distinct results:

1. **Explicit exact theorem:** A pair of terminal weighted projective
   spaces of dimensions 8 and 7, both total weight 51, have matching
   first two supported regularized quantum-period coefficients and
   differing third coefficients.
2. **Computationally exhaustive finite result:** The smallest total
   weight at which two terminal well-formed spaces share the first
   two supported coefficients is 51, and the complete list at 51
   contains 77 pairs, corresponding exactly to partitions of 12.
   Independent implementations are available, but formal verified
   exhaustiveness and external peer review are outstanding.
3. **General finite ambiguity theorem:** For every fixed K, distinct
   terminal well-formed weighted projective spaces can have identical
   first K supported coefficients, even with a known common total
   weight. This follows from prime-valuation nullspace dimension
   and sufficiently many added unit weights, using the prime number
   theorem and the terminality age criterion. It does **not** show
   different dimensions for all K.

Important separation: the full infinite period recovers geometric
dimension in the published weighted-projective result, and the earlier
factorial-factorization reconstruction algorithm is already described
in Veneziale's 2024 thesis. Our finite interpolation argument is a
separate constructive method, not an originality claim.

## Open frontiers

- Optimal degree and dimensional constraints guaranteeing finite
  dimension recovery within terminal spaces.
- Find explicit terminal three-prefix collisions (if any), ideally
  with differing dimensions; no such pair has been found in the
  bounded searches reported here.
- Verify the full source-data pipeline using the published 150,000
  terminal weighted projective spaces.
- Formalize the splitting/padding and nullspace theorems in Lean;
  replay the census with independent formally checked certificates.
- Complete a thorough literature review before any novelty statement.

The state is *research candidate*, not published mathematical novelty.
